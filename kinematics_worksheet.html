PART A Kinematics Activity Sheet
Deriving Kinematic Equation 1
Goal:  Derive the equation \(\displaystyle v = v_0 + at\)
1.
State the Definition of Acceleration
Recall that acceleration is defined as the rate of change of velocity over time. Write the defining formula for average acceleration using delta notation: \[a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}\] Identify and write what each symbol represents: \(\Delta v\) is the change in velocity, and \(\Delta t\) is the elapsed time interval.
2.
Expand the Delta Notation
Using the definitions from Step 1, replace \(\Delta v\) with \((v - v_0)\) and replace \(\Delta t\) with \(t\) (assuming the motion starts at \(t = 0\)). Rewrite the acceleration formula with these substitutions clearly shown: \[a = \frac{v - v_0}{t}\]
3.
Isolate the Final Velocity
Using the equation you arrived at in Step 2, eliminate the fraction by multiplying both sides by \(t\). Write the result, then isolate \(v\) by moving \(v_0\) to the other side. Show each algebraic step on the work area below until you arrive at: \[v = v_0 + at\]

PART B Kinematics Activity Sheet
Deriving Kinematic Equation 4
Goal:  Derive the equation \(\displaystyle x = x_0 + \tfrac{1}{2}(v_0 + v)\,t\)
1.
State the Definition of Average Velocity
Write the defining formula for average velocity as total displacement divided by total elapsed time: \[v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{x - x_0}{t}\] Using this definition, multiply both sides by \(t\) to express displacement as a product, then isolate the final position \(x\) on one side.
2.
Find the Average Velocity Under Constant Acceleration
Under constant acceleration, velocity changes at a uniform (linear) rate. For any quantity that changes linearly, its average value over an interval equals the arithmetic mean of its starting and ending values. Apply this reasoning to write the average velocity as: \[v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{v_0 + v}{2}\]
3.
Substitute and Simplify
Using the position equation you found in Step 1, replace \(v_{\text{avg}}\) with the expression you derived in Step 2. Substitute and simplify to obtain: \[x = x_0 + \tfrac{1}{2}(v_0 + v)\,t\]

PART C Kinematics Activity Sheet
Deriving Kinematic Equation 2
Goal:  Derive the equation \(\displaystyle x = x_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2\)
1.
Identify the Two Source Equations
Write out both equations you will be combining. The first gives velocity as a function of time: \(v = v_0 + at\). The second gives position from average velocity: \(x = x_0 + \tfrac{1}{2}(v_0 + v)\,t\). Label them clearly — you will substitute one into the other in the next step.
2.
Substitute the Velocity Equation
In the position equation from Step 1, locate the variable \(v\). Replace it entirely with the right-hand side of the velocity equation, writing the full substitution explicitly: \[x = x_0 + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[v_0 + (v_0 + at)\bigr]\,t\]
3.
Expand and Simplify
Using the expression you arrived at in Step 2, combine the like terms inside the brackets (\(v_0 + v_0 = 2v_0\)), then distribute \(t\) into the bracket, and finally distribute the factor of \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) to each term. Simplify the coefficients at each sub-step until you reach: \[x = x_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2\]

PART D Kinematics Activity Sheet
Deriving Kinematic Equation 3
Goal:  Derive the equation \(\displaystyle v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)\)
1.
Solve the Velocity Equation for Time
Begin with the first kinematic equation \(v = v_0 + at\). Your goal here is to eliminate time from the derivation. Isolate \(t\) by subtracting \(v_0\) from both sides and then dividing by \(a\). Write the resulting expression for \(t\) clearly before moving to the next step.
2.
Substitute into the Displacement Equation
Write the displacement–average velocity equation: \(x - x_0 = \tfrac{1}{2}(v_0 + v)\,t\). Using the expression for \(t\) you obtained in Step 1, replace \(t\) in this equation with that expression. Write out the substitution in full: \[x - x_0 = \tfrac{1}{2}(v + v_0)\cdot\frac{v - v_0}{a}\]
3.
Apply the Difference of Squares
Using the equation you arrived at in Step 2, combine the two fractions to write: \(x - x_0 = \dfrac{(v + v_0)(v - v_0)}{2a}\). Recognize that the numerator follows the pattern \((a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2\). Apply that identity to simplify the numerator, yielding \(v^2 - v_0^2\).
4.
Isolate the Final Velocity Squared
Using the simplified equation from Step 3, multiply both sides by \(2a\) to clear the denominator. Then move \(v_0^2\) to the right-hand side by adding it to both sides. Show each operation explicitly until you arrive at the final form: \[v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)\]
